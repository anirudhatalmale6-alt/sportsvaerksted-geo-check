Sportsvaerksted, seen from another country

Recorded 5 September 2026 from a machine in Falkenstein, Germany. Six clips: your website and your Facebook page, each visited as an English speaker, as a German speaker, and — as a control — as a Dane.

The connection is genuinely foreign: a German IP address, nowhere near Denmark. The only things set by hand are the browser's language, its regional settings and its clock. That is exactly what a real visitor's browser tells a website about them, so this is what a real visitor gets. The black label in the top-left corner of each clip is mine, added so you can tell the clips apart — nothing else on screen was touched.

What I found

Your website behaves identically no matter where the visitor is from. I compared the full text of the page across all three visits: 67,448 characters each time, not one character different. No redirect, no language switch, no "we noticed you're abroad" popup. Nothing to fix — but also nothing adapting.
The Facebook reviews on your site stay in Danish, and there is no way to read them. The heading around them is English — "FORMER CLIENTS' REVIEWS OF US ON FACEBOOK (260+)", "4.8", "Over 260 Reviews", "Read them here!" — and then the reviews themselves are solid Danish: "Tak for rigtig go' behandling igår…", "Efter utallige besøg hos diverse fysioterapeuter, kiropraktorer og massører…". There is no translate button on them. An English speaker sees a 4.8 rating they cannot check, which is the one thing on the page most likely to convince them.
A logged-out Facebook visitor sees one post, then a wall. Not language — Facebook itself. After a few seconds a "See more from Sportsværkstedet" login box covers the page. Closing it buys a little more scrolling, then the posts below simply stop loading and sit there as empty grey rectangles. Anyone arriving from your website without a Facebook login gets almost nothing.
Facebook does translate its own furniture, and converts your price range. The buttons, tabs and counts follow the visitor: "96% recommend (260 reviews)" / "Von 96 % empfohlen (260 Bewertungen)" / "96 % anbefalet (260 anmeldelser)". Your price band is shown as £ to the English visitor, € to the German one and Billig to the Dane. Your own words — the intro, the posts, the comments — stay in Danish, and logged out Facebook offered no "See translation" link on any of them.

Two things worth knowing

  • I can only arrive from Germany. I have no connections in other countries, so if anything on your site ever reacts to the visitor's country rather than their language, Germany is the only one I can show you. Nothing on your site appears to react to either, so it made no difference here.
  • Your host's firewall blocked me once. My first, non-browser request to sportsvaerksted.com came back as 455 Security Incident Detected from Simply.com's firewall. Every ordinary browser visit went straight through, so this is not affecting your visitors — but it is worth knowing that something is sitting in front of your site making that call.
